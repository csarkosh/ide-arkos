Timo Purple: how far to take it

Why it reads plain next to the others: runic, watercolor, notebook and material each redraw the window's structure (pane edges, tabs, title bar, selection) and give terminal text an entrance (decode, pen, ballpoint). Overworld does neither. It is a small muted picture at the foot of the sidebar, and everything else is two flat purples. Below, the same purple and cyan, the same night overworld, taken three steps further. Each step includes the one before. The mockups move; text stays as crisp as today in all of them.

NOWOverworld as shipped

Flat chrome, the app's own hairline borders, the muted scene, a lantern on the active tab.

1Framed

What differs: every pane, the sidebar and the title bar become game menu windows with notched pixel frames on a dithered night ground (the focused one framed in cyan). The tab bar is a stone ledge, the pane number a gem, the selected file gets a bobbing pixel cursor instead of a flat fill. The title bar is a strip of sky with a drifting cloud. The scene is a little less muted. Still nothing behind any text you read in a tab.

2Framed and alive (my pick)

What differs from 1: the terminal gets its entrance. New text types in like a dialogue box, each fresh character lit cyan-white for a moment (the left pane loops it). A pane whose Claude session is working has a faint glint drifting slowly round its frame (calmed on 12 November: no white head, a third of the speed); a session waiting for you shows the bobbing yellow "continue" arrow in its corner (right pane).

3The whole window is the world (chosen, 12 November)

What differs from 2: the art comes up to nearly full colour, and the night reaches into the panes: a dithered dusk at the top of each body, a few faint stars and a line of hills along its foot, behind the text. This is the step that reopens what you turned down on 4 November (bright art, anything behind text), so it is here to show the ceiling.
